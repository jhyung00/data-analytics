Foundations of Data Literacy

3 Key Questions to Ask


1. What type of data?
Financial, Categorical, Time-series?

2. Communication Goal?
Comparison, Relationship, Distribution?

3. Who is the Audience?
Executive, Manager, Analyst?

Visual Selection Guide

Comparison (Bar/Line)
Use to compare values over time (Line) or across categories (Bar/Column).
Composition (Pie/Stacked)
Use to show parts of a whole. Be careful with Pie charts (max 3-5 slices).
Relationship (Scatter)
Use to show correlations between two variables (e.g., Sales vs Advertising).
Distribution (Histogram)
Use to show frequency of values (e.g., Distribution of Invoice Amounts).

Audience Needs

Needs high-level KPIs & simple cards.

Manager

Needs actionable insights & summaries.

AI in Accounting: Tool or Trap?

How AI Helps

  • Automating reconciliation
  • Drafting memo outlines
  • Summarizing regulations
  • Writing Excel/DAX formulas

The Pitfalls

  • Hallucinations: Inventing standards.
  • Bias: Historical data bias.
  • Data Privacy: Don't upload client data!

Essential Skill: Critically evaluating AI output using domain knowledge.

Cognitive Bias Library

Common biases that affect audit and accounting judgment.

Confirmation Bias

Seeking only evidence that supports your initial belief while ignoring contradictory evidence.

Availability Bias

Relying on information that comes to mind most easily (e.g., recent events) rather than comprehensive data.

Anchoring Bias

Relying too heavily on the first piece of information offered (the "anchor") when making decisions.

Overconfidence

Overestimating one's own ability to perform tasks or to make accurate assessments of risk.

Selection Bias

Selecting data for analysis that is not properly randomized, leading to an unrepresentative sample.

Recency Bias

Giving more weight to the most recent items or events and ignoring historical trends.

The Accounting Analytics Toolchain

Select a tool in the stack to see how it fits into the accounting workflow.

Build the Workflow

1. Alteryx (The Engine)

Role: Heavy Lifting & Automation.

  • Cleans millions of rows of messy ERP data.
  • Joins disparate datasets (e.g., Sales + Shipping Logs).
  • Performs fuzzy matching to find duplicates.

2. Excel (The Workbench)

Role: Logic, Reconciliations & Audit.

  • Validates the "clean" numbers from Alteryx.
  • Calculates complex accounting estimates.
  • Used for ad-hoc "what-if" scenarios.

3. Power BI (The Display)

Role: Monitoring & Storytelling.

  • Visualizes trends for executives.
  • Allows drill-down from Year -> Month -> Transaction.
  • Maintains a "Live Connection" to the data.

Toolchain Quiz

1. Which tool is best for cleaning 5 million rows of messy data?

2. Which tool is best for creating an interactive dashboard for the CEO?

3. Which tool is best for a manual bank reconciliation with complex logic?
